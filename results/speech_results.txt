Speech-Only Emotion Recognition Results

Model Architecture:
MFCC Feature Extraction + BiLSTM + Dense Classifier

Dataset:
Toronto Emotional Speech Set (TESS)

Training Configuration:
- Sampling Rate: 16000 Hz
- MFCC Features: 40
- Batch Size: 32
- Epochs: 10
- Optimizer: Adam
- Learning Rate: 0.001

Results:
Accuracy: 99.82%

Classification Performance:

Emotion      Precision    Recall    F1-score
Angry         1.00         1.00       1.00
Disgust       0.99         1.00       0.99
Fear          1.00         1.00       1.00
Happy         1.00         1.00       1.00
Neutral       1.00         1.00       1.00
Surprise      1.00         0.99       0.99
Sad           1.00         1.00       1.00

Observations:
- The model achieved very high accuracy on the TESS dataset.
- Emotional patterns were effectively captured using MFCC features and BiLSTM temporal modeling.
- Minor misclassification was observed in surprise and disgust classes.
- High accuracy may be influenced by controlled recording conditions and clear emotional expressions in the dataset.


Inference Testing:

Custom Audio Test:
Input Audio: User-recorded speech sample(1.wav)
Predicted Emotion: Surprise

Observation:
The trained speech model successfully generalized to unseen speech input and predicted emotional content from user-provided audio.

Confusion Matrix Analysis:

Observations:
- Angry, Disgust, Neutral, and Sad emotions achieved perfect classification.
- Fear was confused with Angry in one sample.
- Happy was confused with Angry in one sample.
- Surprise was confused with Disgust in one sample.

Analysis:
The confusion occurred between emotions with similar vocal characteristics such as high intensity and strong pitch variations. Overall, the model showed strong separability among emotional classes.

t-SNE Feature Visualization Analysis:

Observations:
- Distinct clusters are formed for different emotional classes.
- Most emotions are clearly separated in feature space.
- Small overlaps exist between certain emotional groups with similar vocal characteristics.

Conclusion:
The extracted MFCC features combined with BiLSTM temporal modeling produced highly discriminative emotional representations.
